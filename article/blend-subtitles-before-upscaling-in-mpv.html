Blend Subtitles Before Upscaling in mpv

In the mpv media player, subtitles are rendered at your display's native resolution after the video has already been scaled by default. However, configuring the blend-subtitles setting forces mpv to rasterize and draw subtitles directly onto video frames before any upscaling, color conversion, or interpolation occurs. This guide explains what the blend-subtitles option does, how to enable it in your configuration, and the visual trade-offs involved.

The Setting: blend-subtitles

The setting that controls this behavior is --blend-subtitles. It accepts three values:

  • no (default): Subtitles are rendered separately at the final screen resolution after the video stream has been processed and upscaled.
  • yes: Blends the subtitle layer directly into the raw video frame before upscaling, interpolation, and color management shaders are applied.
  • video: An alias for yes that ensures the subtitles are drawn at the video's native source resolution.

How to Enable It

To enable subtitle blending via the command line, run:

mpv --blend-subtitles=yes video.mkv

To make this change permanent for all playback, add the following line to your mpv.conf file:

blend-subtitles=yes

Why Use blend-subtitles=yes?

Blending subtitles before upscaling is primarily used to fix visual inconsistencies and synchronization issues:

  • Eliminating Judder with Interpolation: When using mpv's display synchronization and motion interpolation (--interpolation=yes), default subtitles can appear to stutter or judder relative to smooth video motion. Blending ensures subtitles are interpolated alongside video frames at identical timing.
  • Uniform Video Processing: Any custom shaders, color management profiles, or post-processing filters applied to the video chain will also consistently affect the subtitles.

Trade-offs to Consider

Because subtitles are drawn at the video's native resolution rather than the display resolution, watching low-resolution content (such as 480p or 720p) on a high-resolution screen (such as 1440p or 4K) will cause text and styling to be scaled up by your video scaler. This can result in visibly softer, blurry, or pixelated subtitles compared to the crisp text rendered by the default setting.
