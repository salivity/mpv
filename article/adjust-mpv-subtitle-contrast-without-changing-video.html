Adjust mpv Subtitle Contrast Without Changing Video

While mpv does not have a dedicated contrast slider specifically for subtitles like it does for video, you can achieve the exact same effect by modifying subtitle styling. By altering the text color, outline thickness, shadow depth, and background box opacity, you can increase or decrease the visual contrast of text-based subtitles without altering the contrast or color grading of the underlying video.

Text-Based Subtitles (SRT, VTT, ASS)

For standard text-based subtitles, mpv allows you to customize the font appearance directly through the command line or via your mpv.conf configuration file.

To maximize subtitle contrast and readability against bright or busy video backgrounds, apply the following properties:

# Main text color (Format: ARGB or RRGGBB)
sub-color="#FFFFFFFF"

# Border (outline) to separate text from bright backgrounds
sub-border-color="#FF000000"
sub-border-size=3.0

# Shadow for depth and separation
sub-shadow-offset=2.0
sub-shadow-color="#80000000"

# Optional: Add a solid or semi-transparent background box
sub-back-color="#80000000"

Overriding Embedded ASS Styles

If you are watching media with styled ASS/SSA subtitles, mpv defaults to the styles defined by the subtitle creator. To force your own high-contrast styles, add:

sub-ass-override=force

Setting this to force applies your custom borders, colors, and backgrounds to all subtitles, ensuring consistent contrast.

Image-Based Subtitles (PGS, VobSub)

Bitmap or image-based subtitles, commonly found on Blu-ray (PGS) and DVD (VobSub) rips, are pre-rendered graphic overlays rather than text. Because they are rendered as images, text styling options like sub-border-size or sub-color will not apply.

mpv provides limited options to alter the appearance of image-based subtitles independently:

  • Grayscale conversion: Use --sub-gray=yes to convert full-color subtitle images to grayscale, which can reduce clashing colors.
  • General scaling: Use --sub-scale to adjust the size, which may improve legibility against complex scenes.

True contrast adjustments cannot be applied solely to PGS subtitle layers without converting them to text formats (such as SRT via OCR software) first.

Adjusting Contrast Dynamically

To tweak subtitle readability on the fly without editing configuration files every time, you can map subtitle styling toggles in your input.conf file:

# Toggle a high-contrast background box
b cycle-values sub-back-color "#C0000000" "none"

# Increase or decrease outline thickness
k add sub-border-size +0.5
j add sub-border-size -0.5

These shortcuts allow you to adapt the subtitle visibility instantly depending on the scene's brightness without touching the global video contrast settings.
