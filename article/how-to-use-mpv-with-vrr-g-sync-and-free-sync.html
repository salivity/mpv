How to Use mpv with VRR G-Sync and FreeSync

This article provides an overview of how the mpv media player interacts with Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) technologies, such as Nvidia G-Sync and AMD FreeSync. It details the mechanics of VRR playback in mpv, why traditional display-sync options can conflict with adaptive sync, and the exact configuration lines needed to achieve smooth, judder-free video playback.

How mpv Interacts with VRR

Traditional video playback relies on fixed refresh rates. When playing a 23.976 fps film on a standard 60 Hz display, players must use techniques like 3:2 pulldown or frame resampling to bridge the timing mismatch, which often introduces slight stutter or judder.

VRR alters this dynamic by allowing the graphics card to dictate the monitor’s refresh timing frame-by-frame. When configured correctly, mpv presents a frame, and the VRR display waits to refresh until that frame is delivered. This enables native, judder-free playback of any frame rate—such as 23.976, 24, 25, or 50 fps—without manually changing the system display resolution or refresh rate.

Essential mpv Settings for VRR

To allow G-Sync or FreeSync to manage timing, you must avoid mpv options that attempt to lock video frames to an internal, static display clock.

  1. Keep video-sync=audio (Default)
    Do not use --video-sync=display-resample or --video-sync=display-tempo. These modes assume a fixed monitor refresh rate and actively duplicate, drop, or resample audio/video to match that fixed interval. This directly conflicts with VRR, causing erratic refresh rate fluctuations, flickering, or stutter. The default --video-sync=audio allows frames to be displayed as soon as they are ready according to the audio clock.

  2. Ensure Fullscreen Operation
    Most VRR drivers default to operating only in exclusive fullscreen mode. While both Nvidia and AMD offer "Windowed and Fullscreen" VRR modes, windowed VRR can be inconsistent on desktop environments. Use --fullscreen or press f to run mpv in fullscreen to guarantee driver engagement.

  3. Configure the Presentation Mode
    Depending on your operating system and chosen video output (--vo), specific presentation modes yield better VRR stability:

    • Windows (Direct3D 11): Using --gpu-api=d3d11 alongside --d3d11-exclusive-fs=yes forces exclusive fullscreen, ensuring the D3D11 swapchain correctly signals the G-Sync/FreeSync driver.
    • Vulkan: If using --gpu-api=vulkan, set --vulkan-present-mode=fifo (or mailbox depending on your driver's VRR implementation) to maintain proper pacing without tearing.

Low Framerate Compensation (LFC)

Most standard PC monitors have a minimum VRR threshold, typically between 40 Hz and 48 Hz. Because standard cinematic content (24 fps) falls below this range, your hardware relies on Low Framerate Compensation (LFC).

With LFC, the GPU driver automatically doubles or triples the refresh rate (for example, displaying a 24 fps video at 48 Hz or 72 Hz). When using mpv with VRR, ensure LFC is supported by your display over the connection used (DisplayPort is generally more reliable than HDMI for LFC on PC monitors).

Recommended Configuration File (mpv.conf)

Add the following lines to your mpv.conf file to optimize the player for VRR environments:

# Use the default audio-based sync
video-sync=audio

# Start in fullscreen to trigger VRR reliably
fullscreen=yes

# GPU settings (recommended modern setup)
vo=gpu-next
gpu-api=d3d11
d3d11-exclusive-fs=yes

If you are on Linux using Wayland, VRR is handled through the compositor. Ensure adaptive sync is enabled in your desktop environment's display settings (such as in KDE Plasma or GNOME) and that fullscreen=yes is set in mpv.
