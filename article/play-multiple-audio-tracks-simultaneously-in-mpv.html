Play Multiple Audio Tracks Simultaneously in mpv

While mpv does not include an out-of-the-box secondary audio track toggle like it does for secondary subtitles, it is fully capable of playing multiple audio tracks simultaneously for language learning. By leveraging mpv’s integration with FFmpeg’s complex filter graphs (--lavfi-complex), users can mix two or more audio streams together, adjust their relative volumes, or route different languages to individual ear channels.

Identifying Audio Track IDs

Before combining tracks, you must know their stream IDs. Run mpv in your terminal with the target video file to view the track list:

mpv --track-list input_video.mkv

Look for the audio entries (marked as Audio --aid=1, Audio --aid=2, etc.) and note the IDs corresponding to your target language and your native language.

Mixing Two Tracks Concurrently

To mix two audio tracks into a single output, use the amix filter via the --lavfi-complex argument. Replace aid1 and aid2 with the track IDs you found:

mpv input_video.mkv --lavfi-complex="[aid1][aid2]amix=inputs=2[ao]"

In this command:

  • [aid1] and [aid2] represent the chosen audio track inputs.
  • amix=inputs=2 combines the two streams into one.
  • [ao] assigns the mixed stream to mpv's audio output.

Adjusting Relative Volume Levels

For language learners, playing both tracks at full volume can sound cluttered. A common approach is to keep the target language prominent while lowering the native language to serve as background reference:

mpv input_video.mkv --lavfi-complex="[aid1]volume=1.0[a1];[aid2]volume=0.35[a2];[a1][a2]amix=inputs=2[ao]"

Here, volume=1.0 keeps the target language at 100%, while volume=0.35 sets the secondary language to 35% before mixing.

Splitting Audio Channels by Ear

Another effective language learning method is routing one language to the left ear and the other to the right ear, allowing the brain to isolate speech more easily using headphones:

mpv input_video.mkv --lavfi-complex="[aid1]pan=stereo|c0=c0|c1=0[left];[aid2]pan=stereo|c0=0|c1=c0[right];[left][right]amix=inputs=2[ao]"
  • The primary track (aid1) is isolated to the left channel (c0).
  • The secondary track (aid2) is isolated to the right channel (c1).

Automating with input.conf

Entering long filter commands manually each time is inefficient. You can bind dual-audio playback to a shortcut key by adding an entry to your input.conf file:

CTRL+a set lavfi-complex "[aid1]volume=1.0[a1];[aid2]volume=0.3[a2];[a1][a2]amix=inputs=2[ao]"
CTRL+A set lavfi-complex ""

Pressing Ctrl + a activates the dual-track mix, while Ctrl + Shift + a clears the filter to return to default single-track playback.
