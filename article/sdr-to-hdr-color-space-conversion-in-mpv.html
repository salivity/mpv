SDR to HDR Color Space Conversion in mpv

Playing Standard Dynamic Range (SDR) content on a High Dynamic Range (HDR) monitor often leads to washed-out images, incorrect gamma, or oversaturated colors if the conversion is left to the operating system. The most accurate way to handle this in mpv is to leverage the modern gpu-next video output driver alongside proper color space signaling. By instructing mpv to manage the color transform from SDR (Rec.709/BT.1886) to HDR (BT.2020/PQ), you ensure faithful color reproduction and correct black levels without needing to toggle Windows or your monitor out of HDR mode.

1. Use the Modern Render Engine (gpu-next)

The legacy gpu output driver lacks the advanced color management pipeline needed for seamless SDR-to-HDR translation. The modern gpu-next backend relies on libplacebo, which calculates precise colorimetric transformations between different color spaces, gamuts, and transfer characteristics.

Add the following to your mpv.conf:

vo=gpu-next
gpu-api=d3d11 # Use 'vulkan' if preferred or on Linux

2. Enable Native Colorspace Hinting

To prevent double-conversion artifacts caused by the operating system, mpv must communicate directly with your graphics pipeline about what color space the video is using. Setting target-colorspace-hint allows mpv to pass the original or converted metadata directly to the display compositor.

target-colorspace-hint=yes

When enabled on modern platforms like Windows 11 with HDR active, mpv dynamically configures the swapchain to match the content accurately, allowing the OS to blend SDR content properly at the system-defined SDR reference white level.

3. Choose Between Color Accuracy and Dynamic Expansion

There are two approaches to watching SDR on an HDR panel: pure color accuracy (reference playback) or inverse tone mapping (pseudo-HDR).

Method A: Accurate SDR Confinement (Recommended)

This approach places standard Rec.709 content inside the wider BT.2020 container without artificially stretching the highlights or saturation. The video displays exactly as the creator intended.

# Preserves standard gamma and clips non-standard peaks safely
gamut-mapping-mode=clip
tone-mapping=clip

By default, gpu-next targets the monitor's calibrated white point. If SDR content appears too dim against the OS interface, adjust the brightness using the system's "SDR content brightness" slider in your display settings rather than forcing mpv to upscale the luminance.

Method B: Inverse Tone Mapping (Auto-HDR Effect)

If you prefer standard dynamic range content to use the full peak brightness and expanded contrast of your HDR panel, enable inverse tone mapping. This algorithm estimates specular highlights and scales them up into HDR territory.

inverse-tone-mapping=yes
tone-mapping=spline

Recommended mpv.conf Baseline

For the cleanest setup that handles both standard SDR media and native HDR files automatically without manual switching, use the following configuration:

vo=gpu-next
gpu-api=d3d11
target-colorspace-hint=yes
dither-depth=10
gamut-mapping-mode=perceptual

This configuration ensures that mpv correctly translates the 8-bit Rec.709 SDR stream into the 10-bit or 12-bit BT.2020 signaling expected by your HDR screen, avoiding banding, color tinting, and gamma distortion.
