How to Downmix 5.1 Audio to Stereo in mpv

This guide explains how to configure the mpv media player to downmix 5.1 surround sound tracks into a stereo output optimized for headphones. By applying the correct audio settings and matrix filters in your configuration file, you can preserve directional audio cues, keep dialogue clear, and prevent the volume imbalances common when playing multichannel audio through standard two-channel headsets.

Locate or Create the Configuration File

To make persistent changes, edit your mpv.conf file. If the file does not exist, create it in the appropriate directory for your operating system:

  • Windows: %APPDATA%\mpv\mpv.conf (or inside the portable_config folder in your mpv directory)
  • Linux: ~/.config/mpv/mpv.conf
  • macOS: ~/.config/mpv/mpv.conf

Method 1: The Standard Stereo Downmix

For general stereo downmixing without dynamic range loss or clipping, add the following lines to your mpv.conf:

# Force stereo output
audio-channels=stereo

# Normalize audio to prevent clipping when multiple channels are mixed
audio-normalize-downmix=yes

This ensures that mpv requests a stereo output from its audio backend and uses libswresample to fold all channels into two without exceeding 0 dBFS.

Method 2: Custom Matrix Downmix (Clearer Dialogue)

The default downmixing algorithm can sometimes result in quiet dialogue because the center channel is attenuated too heavily. You can specify an explicit audio matrix using FFmpeg's pan audio filter to boost the center and surround presence:

audio-channels=stereo
af="pan=stereo|FL < 0.5*FC + 0.707*FL + 0.707*BL + 0.5*LFE|FR < 0.5*FC + 0.707*FR + 0.707*BR + 0.5*LFE"
  • FL/FR: Front Left / Front Right
  • FC: Front Center (dialogue)
  • BL/BR: Back Left / Back Right (surround effects)
  • LFE: Low-Frequency Effects (subwoofer)

The < symbol tells the filter to normalize the mixing levels automatically to avoid distortion.

Method 3: Binaural Virtual Surround (Headphone Optimized)

To achieve a true spatial surround effect over stereo headphones, use FFmpeg's sofalizer or headphone filter via mpv’s lavfi wrapper. The simplest built-in virtual spatializer uses the extrastereo and crossfeed filters, or the native libbs2b library:

audio-channels=stereo
af=lavfi=[stereotools=mutel=0:muter=0:phase=0:slev=1.4:sbal=0:mpan=0]

Alternatively, you can generate true spatial downmixing using FFmpeg's headphone filter with synthetic Impulse Response profiles:

audio-channels=stereo
af=lavfi=[headphone=map=FL|FR|FC|LFE|BL|BR]

This calculates interaural time differences (ITD) and interaural level differences (ILD) to simulate 5.1 speakers in a virtual 3D room directly through your headphones.

Verifying Your Setup

To confirm that the downmixing works during playback:

  1. Open your 5.1 audio video file in mpv.
  2. Press Shift + I to open the statistics overlay.
  3. Press 2 on your keyboard to navigate to the Audio/Video details page.
  4. Verify that the Audio Filter line shows your specified downmix parameters and the output format displays stereo (2 ch).
